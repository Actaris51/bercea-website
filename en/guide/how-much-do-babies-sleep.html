How much do babies sleep?
The chart by age

📖 5 min read 🔬 WHO / AASM guidelines Updated: October 2026

14 hours? 11 hours? With or without naps? A baby's sleep needs change enormously over the months, and two babies of the same age can be far apart. Here are the research-based reference points, and above all how to read them.

💡 How to read this chart: the ranges are wide, on purpose. A 6-month-old who sleeps 12 hours is as normal as one who sleeps 15. What matters is the regularity of their rhythm and how they are during the day (mood, appetite, quality awake time).

Age by age, in detail: 3 months, 4 months, 6 months, 9 months and 12 months.

Before 3 months: forget the clock

Newborns live on an ultradian rhythm: short cycles of 3 to 4 hours driven by hunger, independent of day and night. The melatonin passed on by the mother during pregnancy is gone, and the baby's own production only starts around 8 to 12 weeks.

At this age, talking about "bedtime" makes little sense. What you can do: expose your baby to natural daylight, dim the lights in the evening, and let the circadian rhythm settle in at its own pace.

3 to 6 months: the big shift

This is the turning point. Sleep matures, melatonin kicks in and nights start to consolidate. It's also when good habits pay off most: a regular bedtime routine and putting baby down at the right time, within the wake window for their age.

"Sleeping through the night": what it really means

In studies, "sleeping through" means 5 to 6 hours in a row, not 12 hours without a sound. It becomes physiologically possible around 3 to 4 months, but:

  • Brief wakings between cycles happen at every age (adults too)
  • The real skill isn't never waking up, it's falling back asleep alone
  • Between 6 and 12 months, nearly 8 babies in 10 still wake at least once a night, and that is normal

Warning signs

See your doctor if your baby persistently sleeps well below the lower ranges, is hard to wake, constantly drowsy, or if sleep comes with loud snoring, breathing pauses or a drop in their growth curve.
